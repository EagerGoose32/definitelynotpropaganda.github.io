The Clock Tower X Network: Mapping a $9M Foreign Influence Operation Targeting American Phones

01 / How This Started

On June 30, 2026, an unsolicited text arrived from a number identifying itself as "Jenn" with an organization called Friends for Peace:

Text Receipt — +1 703-289-0571
"Hi, I'm Jenn with Friends for Peace. A new agreement could weaken an Iran-backed terrorist group without more U.S. troops. Open to chat? Stop2End"

A second, separate number — +1 703-520-5751 — sent a near-identical script under the name "James" from an org called Partners in Peace, with the same "Stop2End" footer. When pushed to get on a call, the response was immediate and revealing:

Text Receipt — +1 703-520-5751
"We are unable to make calls right now but I am happy to chat more over text."

Neither "Friends for Peace" nor "Partners in Peace" exist as registered organizations. They are not findable in any nonprofit registry, FEC filing, or FARA database under those names — because those names are disposable wrappers. Investigative outlet Responsible Statecraft documented the same pattern across four separate numbers reaching Americans in California, Florida, Texas, Wisconsin, and Alabama, sometimes in Spanish, since November 2025.

Responsible Statecraft, Feb 9, 2026
"During one text conversation, the campaign admitted that they 'use different names' for the organization."
responsiblestatecraft.org/parscale-pro-israel-texts

02 / Follow the Invoice

The texting operation traces to Clock Tower X LLC, a firm founded by former Trump 2020 campaign manager Brad Parscale, operating under a direct contract with the Israeli government.

Responsible Statecraft
"Clock Tower X's contract with the Israeli Ministry of Foreign Affairs, which increased from $6 million to $9 million in December, includes 'Delivery of monthly updated audience segmentation and sentiment analysis, including Gen Z and other key U.S. demographic groups.'"

The operation is internally branded "Project 545," per public statements from Israeli MFA chief of staff Eran Shayovich, Parscale's direct point of contact in Israel:

Eran Shayovich, LinkedIn (cited by RS)
"A year and two months into a long war, when the image of the State of Israel was at one of its lowest points... we began to fight back seriously."

The same contract extends into earned and paid media well beyond text messages — CTX is also integrating pro-Israel messaging into Salem Media Network, the conservative media conglomerate behind shows like "The Right View with Lara Trump" and "The Dinesh D'Souza Podcast."

03 / The Network: Mapped Domains

Per journalist Jack Poulson's reporting, Clock Tower X operates at least 11 distinct websites. This investigation has independently confirmed and documented the following nodes, all sharing the identical FARA footer disclaimer and @clocktowerx.com contact infrastructure:

allyvia.org
Hard policy/security front. "Strengthening the US and Israel Alliance for Security, Prosperity & Shared Values." Hosts the "Truth Hub" — see Section 04.
culturavia.org
Soft cultural/faith front. "Faith • Freedom • Friendship." Targets audiences who'd bounce off hard security framing. See Section 05.
YouTube: "Allies for Peace"
Video arm. Channel created two weeks before the SMS campaign launched. Hosts the edited Joe Rogan/Douglas Murray "Tunnels" clip pushed directly in text conversations.

Both confirmed sites carry the identical legally-mandated disclosure, present only in the page footer:

Footer text, allyvia.org & culturavia.org
"This material is distributed by Clock Tower X LLC on behalf of the State of Israel. Additional information is available at the Department of Justice, Washington, DC."
The Gap
This disclosure exists on the websites. It does not exist in the text messages that drive traffic to those websites. A FARA attorney consulted by Responsible Statecraft noted the law requires a "conspicuous statement" disclosing foreign-agent status within the actual outreach — not three clicks away in a footer nobody reads.

04 / Case Study: The "Truth Hub" That Fails Its Own Test

Allyvia hosts a page literally titled "How to Spot Propaganda." It instructs readers to watch for:

  • "Emotional manipulation — using graphic imagery or inflammatory language to provoke reaction before critical thinking engages."
  • "Does the post cite credible organizations or provide verifiable evidence?"
  • "Decontextualized photos and videos... a reverse image search often reveals original context."
  • "Be suspicious of claims lacking specific dates, locations, or attribution."
  • "Consider timing and coordination. Disinformation campaigns often involve multiple accounts posting identical or nearly identical content simultaneously."

Every bullet describes the site's own operation. The adjacent "Truth Hub" page makes unsourced claims throughout — "legal scholars note," "military analysts," "Hamas's documented practice" — with zero named sources, zero links, zero dates. The site that warns you to distrust content lacking attribution is, itself, content lacking attribution.

05 / Case Study: Culturavia, the Softer Front

Culturavia is structurally distinct from Allyvia — same operator, same FARA footer, different audience segment. Where Allyvia leads with security and policy, Culturavia leads with "Faith & Heritage," "Arts & Culture," and "Philanthropy." It is also a registered fundraising vehicle:

Culturavia Privacy Policy
"All purchases from the online store are considered contributions to CTX." / "Information as required by the Oklahoma Ethics Commission... requires us to collect and disclose the name and mailing address, occupation and employer of all individuals whose donations exceed $200 per calendar year."
culturavia.org/legal/privacy-policy — mailing address listed: 3745 Medina Rd., Ste C., Medina, OH 44256-9510

Culturavia's page on antisemitism, "Distinguishing Legitimate Criticism of Israel from Antisemitism," is notably more careful than Allyvia's content — it accurately quotes the IHRA Working Definition and lists genuinely protected criticism (opposing settlement policy, advocating Palestinian statehood, criticizing military operations). That accuracy is itself worth flagging: it never mentions that IHRA's own lead drafter, Kenneth Stern, has publicly objected to the definition being weaponized against campus speech, nor does it mention the Jerusalem Declaration on Antisemitism or the Nexus Document were created specifically because scholars disputed how IHRA was being applied. The page also never engages the hardest case — how the "double standards" clause gets operationalized against BDS legislation and ICC/ICJ proceedings — sticking instead to the easiest, least contested examples.

Read
The two sites appear calibrated by audience: Allyvia's harder framing gets the crude strawmen; Culturavia's "faith and culture" framing gets the more careful, harder-to-catch omissions. Same operator, different dosing.

06 / The Paper Trail

Allyvia's own Mobile Terms & Conditions document drops the "Friends for Peace" / "Partners in Peace" cover entirely and signs as the operating company directly:

allyvia.org/legal/mobile-terms-conditions
"CLOCK TOWER X LLC ('Clock Tower,' 'CTX,' 'We,' 'Us,' 'Our') is offering a mobile messaging program... you agree to receive recurring autodialed marketing mobile messages and you understand that your consent to be contacted in this method is not required to make any purchase from Us."

This is the company's own legal classification of the texts: autodialed marketing. Not civic dialogue. Not grassroots peace outreach. Marketing, run by a foreign government's contracted PR firm, sent to American phones under invented charity names.

Archived snapshot, mobile T&C page (2026.06.30): archive.today/2026.06.30-222730

07 / The Pattern, Named

This is Zone Flooding executed as a vertically integrated operation: SMS outreach generates initial contact → bot/script holds engagement → links push to "Truth Hub" content for pre-loaded skepticism toward Gaza reporting → cultural-affinity site (Culturavia) builds soft long-term trust → all under a $9M government contract with disclosure technically present but functionally invisible.

None of the individual facts deployed across these sites are necessarily fabricated outright. The dishonesty is structural: real facts, arranged and scoped to imply conclusions the facts alone don't support, with the actual contested evidence — ICJ intent findings, the Nakba, the British Mandate, MoH casualty cross-validation, the IHRA/JDA scholarly split — consistently absent. It is not crude lying. It is omission as methodology, at industrial scale, run by a former presidential campaign manager on a seven-figure foreign government retainer.

The Nov. 13 Ground Stop: What Broke, What Was Pushed Out Three Days Earlier, and Who Gets Paid

An AI routing tool sent nationwide before its own test ended, a written warning nobody answered, a contractor-run network that reportedly crashed after an update, and a $30.1 billion funding ask already on appropriators' desks.

Field documentation · November 14, 2026 · DNP Operations Archive · Drafted with AI assistance (Claude)

Three days before Friday's nationwide ground stop, the FAA pushed its new AI routing tool, SMART, to every en route center in the country. It had not finished its own 90-day test, and 25 members of Congress had asked in writing how it would hold up during an equipment outage. The day before the crash, Duffy and OMB Director Russ Vought sent appropriators a letter asking for $30.1 billion. Then the L3Harris network that carries controller radio traffic reportedly went down after an update. By that night Duffy was blaming 1980s copper and asking for $50 billion.

Not Established
None of that proves the outage was planned, and nothing in the reporting reviewed for this entry says SMART caused it. What it does show is an agency that skipped its own safety homework, ignored a warning about this exact kind of failure, and had the invoice drafted before anything broke.

02 / The AI Tool That Skipped Its Own Test

SMART is not a robot flying planes. It is a recommendation engine. It pulls in about 200 data feeds, such as weather, traffic and airspace restrictions, and suggests routing and scheduling changes. Controllers decide whether to take them, and the FAA has been clear it cannot take control of an aircraft.

The problem is how fast it went out the door. Boston-based Air Space Intelligence (ASI) won the $875 million, 12-year contract on June 22, beating Palantir and Thales. The deal also covers FMDS, the new data backbone for the FAA's national command center. SMART went live around DC on Sept. 21 as the start of a 90-day evaluation, and Duffy had talked about full deployment by spring 2027. On Nov. 10, about 50 days in, the FAA pushed it to all 22 en route centers.

That came after the Oct. 9 letter asking for a pause and for details on how SMART would perform during equipment outages. The deadline was Oct. 19. The FAA expanded anyway without answering publicly. NATCA, the controllers' union, says it was never involved in the design, testing or rollout.

ASI says the ground stop had nothing to do with SMART, "any product of ASI or our integration with FAA and ATC." It posted that Friday night, before anyone official had pointed at it.

Secondhand — Confirm Before Citing
The money around ASI is still secondhand reporting. Treat each item below as a lead to confirm, not a finding.
  • The FAA wants to own part of ASI. Politico, citing an internal FAA document, reported a term sheet for an equity stake and warrants. It would be the first time the FAA has sought one. Not finalized as of mid-October.
  • Valuation jump. ASI was valued at about $300 million in December 2023. In October 2026, four months after the award, it raised $325 million at a $3 billion valuation. Investors include Andreessen Horowitz, Spark Capital and Citadel Securities affiliates.
  • The son-in-law donation. ASI's PAC reportedly gave $2,500 in March 2026 to Michael Alfonso, Duffy's son-in-law and a Wisconsin congressional candidate. That was about three months before the award. Sourcing is secondary (The Bulwark, WisPolitics). The same writeup says there is no evidence it affected the award.
The Gap
What this investigation cannot show yet is a technical link. SMART pulls a lot of data, and Duffy says the network choked on data, but nobody has shown SMART traffic runs over the L3Harris radio network that failed. Until someone does, it is timing, not cause.

03 / The Network That Has Failed Before

The best-sourced explanation so far is the boring one, which is usually a good sign. The Wall Street Journal reported the outage was hitting a telecom network overseen by L3Harris, and multiple sources told CBS that an update to that network made it reboot and crash. It is the FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure network, or FTI, which carries the radio frequencies controllers use to talk to pilots. L3Harris had not commented as of Friday night, and no official has confirmed the update story.

The FAA does not even own this network. Harris won FTI in 2002, and the DOT Inspector General described the contract as basically a lease. L3Harris also runs the FAA's Starlink testing under the same program.

The record of failures

YearWhat failedSource
2009 A single router in Salt Lake City took down other FAA systems and caused delays nationwide. Nextgov
2006 A scheduled Harris software update knocked out lines at O'Hare. The technicians on site called it entirely avoidable. Route Fifty

After 2009 the Inspector General warned that critical outages would stay a risk as new NextGen services got added to FTI. That is close to what Mullin described Friday.

Two things this investigation cannot nail down yet:

  • Whether Duffy's "routers overloaded with data" and CBS's "an update caused a reboot" are the same event told two ways, or two different stories.
  • Peraton, the prime contractor running the whole rebuild, used to be part of Harris. That is corporate family history, not evidence of anything.

04 / Three Explanations in One Night

Officials gave three versions on Friday, and none of them names what actually set it off.

Duffy said the routers started giving inconsistent data, so they shut the system down. Then he corrected himself mid-answer.

Duffy, Nov. 13 press conference
"got overloaded with data" / "I shouldn't say they went down." / "we use 1980s little copper lines"
CBS News · Gizmodo

He also said none of the new equipment went down.

Mullin told it differently. He said the patches and new equipment were overwhelming the older system:

Mullin, Nov. 13 press conference
"overwhelming the older system."
Gizmodo

That puts the upgrade work in the chain of events, which is a very different story from old wires dying of old age. Then there are CBS's sources pointing at an L3Harris update.

Duffy, on the cause and on what comes next
"We're still working on the details of what went wrong today." / "You will have more of them"
Axios · Latin Times (the second quote, in answer to CNN)

Mullin ruled out a cyberattack and any link to the Microsoft outage, but that is their position, not a finding. Asked by CNN why the FAA was not ready, Duffy's answer was that there will be more of them unless Congress tells the agency to build.

Read
The gap between Duffy and Mullin is the most useful thing on the record. "Old gear failed" sells the funding bill. "New gear overwhelmed old gear" means somebody installed something, and somebody signed off on it.

05 / Follow the Invoice

The $30.1 billion ask was already sitting with appropriators the day before the outage. By Friday night it was $50 billion.

This is the second time an outage and a funding push have landed within a day of each other. The Nov. 12 letter is the sharpest timing fact in this whole thing. It does not prove intent, but it does prove the ask was being worked before the crash, not built because of it.

As for who gets paid: Peraton is the prime integrator on the whole rebuild, and its CEO thanked Trump, Duffy and Bedford by name when it won. L3Harris runs FTI, the network that reportedly crashed, and telecom upgrade money runs through that program. ASI's FAA ceiling is $875.9 million, but only about $23.3 million had actually been committed, per research firm Altis. The rest depends on future money, including that $600 million SMART line. ASI also picked up an Army deal worth up to $99 million and a $26 million sole-source Air Force order in September.

So flip the question. Would the rush look any different without the money? A vendor with $852 million still on the table has every reason to want a fast, visible nationwide launch before appropriations.

06 / Was It on Purpose?

The record does not support it, and this investigation does not claim it.

What is on paper: the funding ask was ready before the outage, and it went from $30.1 billion to $50 billion the same night. SMART went nationwide before its own evaluation was done, over a written warning nobody publicly answered.

What is reported but not confirmed: unnamed sources told CBS and the Journal that a network update triggered the crash. SMART playing any role is unproven. The timing and the footprint line up, but nothing connects it to the FTI network. And there is no evidence at all that anyone did this deliberately.

The "deliberate" version is also the weakest one to run with. It needs FAA techs, L3Harris staff, ASI and the controllers to all keep quiet, and NATCA is already saying publicly that it got shut out. It cannot be disproven, so every denial turns into more proof. And it hands Duffy an easy out: call it a conspiracy theory and skip the questions he cannot answer, like why the evaluation got cut short.

Read
The version that holds up is worse for them anyway. A rushed contractor rollout ran into a fragile leased network after they had been warned in writing about exactly that. Then they used the wreckage to sell a bill they had already sent.

07 / What to Pull Next

Five questions would settle most of this:

  1. What exactly changed on the FTI network on Nov. 13, and when, compared to 3:30 p.m. ET?
  2. Who signed off on taking SMART nationwide before the 90-day evaluation ended, and on what criteria?
  3. Did SMART or FMDS data run over the FTI circuits that failed?
  4. Did the FAA ever answer the Oct. 9 letter?
  5. When was the Nov. 12 funding letter drafted, and was it timed to anything?
